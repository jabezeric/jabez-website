🌍 Climate Change

Causes and Effects of Climate Change

Introduction to Global Warming

Fossil fuels – coal, oil and gas – are by far the largest contributor to global climate change, accounting for around 68 per cent of global greenhouse gas emissions and nearly 90 per cent of all carbon dioxide emissions. As greenhouse gas emissions blanket the Earth, they trap the sun's heat. Because of this, the planet is warming up and the climate is changing. The world is now warming faster than at any point in recorded history.

Major Drivers and Human Causes

⚡ Electricity and Heat Generation

One of the major causes of climate change is the generation of electricity and heat through the burning of fossil fuels. Coal, oil, and natural gas remain the primary sources of energy in many countries. When these fuels are burned to generate electricity and heat, they release massive amounts of greenhouse gases into the atmosphere, making this sector a significant contributor to global warming.

🏭 Manufacturing and Industrial Output

Manufacturing and industrial activities contribute significantly to climate change. Industries require enormous amounts of energy to produce products such as cement, steel, iron, plastics, electronics, and chemicals. These production processes emit substantial quantities of greenhouse gases, and industrial waste further exacerbates environmental degradation.

🌲 Deforestation and Land Use Changes

Deforestation is another major cause of climate change. Forests absorb carbon dioxide from the atmosphere and store carbon in trees and vegetation. When forests are cleared for farming, livestock grazing, urban development, and logging, they lose their ability to sequester carbon. Additionally, the cleared land often becomes a source of emissions rather than a carbon sink.

🚗 Global Transportation Systems

Transportation is an important source of greenhouse gas emissions. Most cars, buses, trucks, ships, and airplanes depend on gasoline, diesel, or other fossil fuels. Burning these fuels releases carbon dioxide and other pollutants into the atmosphere. As global transportation demands increase, so do its climate-related impacts.

🌾 Agricultural and Food Production

Food production plays a significant role in climate change. Agricultural activities generate greenhouse gases through livestock farming, fertilizer use, land clearing, and the operation of farm machinery. Livestock production is particularly carbon-intensive, as cattle and other animals produce methane, a potent greenhouse gas.

🏢 Commercial and Residential Buildings

Buildings contribute to climate change because homes, offices, and commercial buildings consume large amounts of electricity for heating, cooling, lighting, and operating appliances. Where electricity is generated from fossil fuels, buildings become significant contributors to overall greenhouse gas emissions.

♻️ Unsustainable Patterns of Consumption

Unsustainable patterns of consumption contribute to climate change. Everyday activities such as using electricity, driving vehicles, purchasing goods, and generating waste increase greenhouse gas emissions. Our throwaway culture and overconsumption drive demand for energy-intensive production and transportation.

Global Environmental and Social Effects

🌡️ Escalating Global Temperatures

Climate change has already produced serious environmental, economic, and social consequences across the world. Rising global temperatures are among the most visible effects. The last decade has been the warmest on record, with unprecedented heatwaves affecting communities worldwide. Higher temperatures alter ecosystems, threaten wildlife habitats, and pose direct risks to human health.

⛈️ Intensification of Severe Storms

Another major effect is the increase in severe storms. Warmer temperatures cause more water to evaporate into the atmosphere, leading to heavier rainfall and increased flooding. Warmer oceans also provide more energy for hurricanes and typhoons, intensifying their destructive power. Extreme weather events are becoming more frequent and severe.

🏜️ Widespread and Prolonged Droughts

Climate change is increasing the frequency and severity of droughts. Higher temperatures reduce water availability in many regions, making freshwater supplies less reliable. Agricultural droughts threaten food production and livestock farming, while water droughts affect billions of people who depend on consistent water supplies for drinking, irrigation, and industry.

🌊 Extreme Shifts in World Oceans

The world's oceans are undergoing major changes as a result of climate change. Oceans absorb most of the excess heat generated by global warming, causing ocean temperatures to rise. Warmer water expands, leading to sea level rise that threatens coastal communities and islands. Ocean acidification also endangers marine ecosystems and the species that depend on them.

🦁 Severe Threats to Global Biodiversity

Climate change threatens biodiversity on an unprecedented scale. Rising temperatures, changing habitats, wildfires, invasive species, and extreme weather events are driving many plant and animal species toward extinction. Entire ecosystems are at risk of collapse as species struggle to adapt to rapidly changing conditions. The loss of biodiversity undermines the resilience of natural systems that support all life on Earth.
