🌍 Climate Change

Causes and Effects of Climate Change

Climate change

Climate change is driven primarily by human activities like burning fossil fuels, deforestation, and industrial farming, resulting in profound global effects such as rising temperatures, melting ice sheets, and extreme weather.

Understanding Climate Change

Climate change describes long-term shifts in global temperatures and weather patterns. While Earth’s climate has experienced natural variations over millions of years due to solar cycles and volcanic eruptions, the rapid warming observed since the mid-20th century is overwhelmingly driven by human activity. This human-induced process rapidly alters the natural balance of gases in our atmosphere, creating an enhanced greenhouse effect that traps excessive heat.

The Core Causes

The primary driver of modern climate change is the human expansion of the natural greenhouse effect. Certain atmospheric gases act like a blanket, trapping heat radiating from the Earth and preventing it from escaping back into space.

Burning Fossil Fuels

Generating electricity, powering transport, and running factories by burning coal, oil, and gas release massive amounts of carbon dioxide (CO₂). Data monitored by the NASA Climate Vital Signs Portal shows atmospheric CO₂ has surpassed 420 parts per million. The transportation sector alone—including cars, ships, and airplanes—accounts for nearly one-quarter of global energy-related emissions, as petroleum-based fuels remain the dominant energy source for mobility.

Deforestation

Forests act as vital carbon sinks, absorbing enormous quantities of CO₂ from the air. When trees are cleared for agriculture or urban expansion, this stored carbon is released, compounding atmospheric buildup. Currently, tropical deforestation contributes roughly 10% of annual global greenhouse gas emissions.

Agricultural Practices

Livestock farming—particularly cattle and sheep production—generates high levels of methane (CH₄), a potent heat-trapping gas emitted during digestion. Additionally, synthetic nitrogen fertilizers release nitrous oxide into the soil and atmosphere, which is nearly 300 times more effective at trapping heat than CO₂ over a century.

Industrial Processes

Manufacturing materials like steel, cement, electronics, and plastics consumes vast amounts of energy and frequently emits specialized greenhouse gases, including hydrofluorocarbons used in refrigeration and air conditioning.

The Widespread Effects

As greenhouse gas concentrations climb, the planetary system responds with broad, destructive changes documented across ecosystems and human societies.

Rising Temperatures

According to the NASA Science Climate Change Evidence Guide, the average global surface temperature has risen about 2 degrees Fahrenheit (over 1°C) since the late 19th century, fueling prolonged heatwaves that strain public health systems and reduce labor productivity in outdoor sectors.

Melting Ice and Rising Seas

Polar ice sheets, glaciers, and Arctic sea ice are melting at an accelerated pace. This influx of water, combined with the thermal expansion of warming oceans, causes sea levels to rise, threatening coastal communities and low-lying island nations. Furthermore, the thawing of permafrost in northern regions releases additional methane, creating a dangerous feedback loop that accelerates warming further.

Extreme Weather Events

Warmer air holds more moisture, leading to severe downpours, flash floods, and intense tropical storms. Conversely, other regions experience long-lasting, severe droughts and devastating wildfires, which destroy homes and degrade air quality across continents.

Biodiversity Loss

Research published in the IPCC Sixth Assessment Report details how rapid climate shifts force species extinction and irreversible loss of biodiversity in ecosystems like coral reefs and Arctic regions. Ocean acidification—caused by seas absorbing excess CO₂—simultaneously weakens shellfish and coral skeletons, disrupting the entire marine food web.

Human Health, Food Security, and Economic Stability

The World Health Organization Fact Sheet warns that climate change causes approximately 250,000 additional deaths per year from undernutrition, malaria, diarrhoea, and heat stress due to disrupted food systems and shifting pathogen ranges. Beyond health, economic damages from crop failures, infrastructure destruction, and supply-chain interruptions are projected to cost trillions annually by mid-century if emissions remain unchecked, underscoring the urgent need for coordinated global action.
